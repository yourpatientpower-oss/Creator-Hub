## 1. Question investigated

What harm (documented and potential), responses, oversight and formal findings are on the public record for:
- the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant (IAAP), EPA ID IA7213820445;
- its 2018 RCRA permit (S1);
- the November 2026 emergency permit for in-place detonation of lead azide (S2);
- the operator, American Ordnance LLC (AO)?

## 2. Findings

### A. Environmental and health harm (site-wide; context for C13, C14, C15)

**Documented harm (agency records)**

1. **Superfund listing and contamination profile.**
   - IAAP was proposed for the NPL on 1989-07-14 and listed as final on 1990-08-30 (S140).
   - EPA's site profile lists the primary contaminants of concern as explosives (RDX), metals, VOCs and depleted uranium (S100, S101).
   - RDX is documented in on-post soil, surface water and groundwater, and in **off-post groundwater** (S101).
   - The 1998 OU1 ROD attributes contamination mainly to explosives-laden wastewater and sludge discharged to uncontrolled lagoons, plus open burning and landfilling (S136).
   - USACE states that "some private properties near IAAAP have been impacted by contaminated groundwater and surface water" (S105).
2. **Private wells.**
   - ATSDR (1999) records RDX in five off-site private wells. It was first detected in September 1992, at a maximum of 27.5 ppb against the 2 ppb health advisory (S102).
   - Residences south of the plant were connected to rural water (S102), and EPA says affected residents were connected to an alternative supply (S100, S101).
   - Off-site aquifers still supply private production and irrigation wells (S137).
3. **Ongoing scope (EPA site profile, S100).**
   - 13 operable units.
   - OU6, 7 and 10–13 are still in RI/FS.
   - Construction Complete = No; Sitewide Ready for Anticipated Use = No (S101).
4. **Depleted uranium.** FUSRAP excavation of DU-contaminated soil at the Firing Sites Area (OU8) is ongoing and expected to finish late 2026 or early 2027 (S100). The Iowa Department of Public Health oversees radiological matters (S140).
5. **PFAS (C14).**
   - EPA records PFAS in groundwater at OU4, the former and new fire stations and the fire-training areas "as of July 2022". A dedicated OU13 is in RI/FS (S100, S101).
   - This postdates the 2018 permit. The permit's silence on PFAS (C14) is therefore an omission relative to *later* knowledge. No source found shows PFAS data existed before September 2018.
6. **Remedy protectiveness.** The Army's RAB minutes report that the **2021 Five-Year Review** found OU1 soil remediation goals for antimony, cadmium, hexavalent chromium, thallium and mercury "no longer protective", requiring a post-ROD change (S104). The FYR itself was not retrieved, and the 2003/2008/2013/2018/2023 protectiveness statements were not captured.
7. **EPA performance indicators (as displayed 2026-11-25).**
   - **Human Exposure Under Control: Yes.**
   - **Groundwater Migration Under Control: Yes** (S101).
   - The page does not show the determination dates.
8. **The 2018 permit itself (S1 L2836–2840, L3232, L3606) records** that "The Demolition Area has groundwater contamination migrating beyond area boundaries with releases to surface water." This is an agency statement of an existing release at the area containing the OB/OD units.

**Health findings (ATSDR; no other health claims are made here)**

9. **ATSDR 1999 PHA (S102)** classified the site "No Apparent Public Health Hazard". It also:
   - rated **past** exposure to on- and off-site surface water an **indeterminate** public health hazard;
   - stated that no quantitative measure of exposure exists for the period before 1985.
10. **ATSDR 2013 Letter Health Consultation (S103).** Written in response to resident concerns. RDX and nitrate exceeded comparison values in off-site surface water and/or private well water. The conclusion section was **not captured**, so no outcome is reported here.

**Responses**

11. Alternate water supply for affected residents (S100–S102).
12. Groundwater treatment and monitored natural attenuation, land-use controls, and soil excavation (several hundred thousand cubic yards) (S100, S105).

### B. OB/OD air emissions and community concern (C6, C8, C15)

1. **Regulator acknowledgment (C6, C8).**
   - EPA's 2018 Response to Comments says the OB/OD risk assessment dates from 2007, and that a new one "will be performed once funding through the U.S. Army is approved" (S1 L100–101).
   - Under Conditions V.J, VI.I and VII.I, a new air risk assessment is **discretionary** ("the Director may require") and is tied to release-detection monitoring (S1 L2900–2910).
   - No public record of a post-2007 OB/OD risk assessment was found. **Absence is not proof**: this was a search of public web sources only.
2. **Iowa DNR Air Quality Bureau** commented that the draft permit could imply the facility no longer had to follow Iowa open-burning rules. EPA removed the language (S1 RtC #4). This is a **response**, not a finding of harm.
3. **Air permits.** Iowa DNR lists a Title V major-source permit (29-01-004). It recorded a 2022 Title V inspection with "No Deficiencies Noted" and a 2014 visit coded "LNC" (meaning not verified) (S115).
4. **Community concerns.**
   - The 2018 public commenter raised groundwater and air toxics (S1 RtC #1–3).
   - In 2026, residents, two NGOs, the Iowa City manager and officials in Iowa and Illinois raised concerns about the lead plume (S117, S122).
   - These are **allegations or concerns**, not findings. No review sites or forums were mined.

### C. Worker harm

1. **EEOICPA (documented, quantified).**
   - A Special Exposure Cohort covers DOE/AEC contractor employees at IAAP Line 1 from March 1949 through 1974 (250 work-day minimum) (S108). It took effect on 2005-06-19, per a DOL decision excerpt.
   - DOL statistics (as-of date **not captured**; live page now 404) show for "Iowa Ordnance Plant": 7,083 claims, 4,077 cases, **$238,898,448 compensation + $133,396,044 medical = $372,294,492** (S109). IPR reports "nearly $400 million" (S116).
   - Status: these are **agency compensation determinations** under a no-fault program. The awards are not findings of fault against any contractor.
2. **OSHA (regulatory citations; final orders where noted).**
   - **2006**, inspection 301555504 (S110): 9 serious citations, mostly process safety management (1910.119). Penalty $40,500, reduced to $34,000 via informal settlement.
   - **2019**: $10,000 for recordkeeping/reporting (1904.29, 1904.39). Reportedly a hospitalized worker was reported late (S111, S112).
   - **2024**, inspection 347498107 (S111): 1 serious citation for respiratory protection (1910.134(d)(1)(iii)). Penalty $9,795, reduced to $5,877, final order. News describes it as failing to evaluate at least 20 workers for TNT exposure (S112).
   - The 2006 record names AO as the establishment. A news report says AO has operated IAAP only "since 2008" (S112). This is unresolved; refer to timeline-analyst and money-mapper.
3. **Accidents.**
   - An unplanned explosion in January 2025 with no injuries reported (S116, citing the Des Moines Register).
   - A former worker's recollection of 1960s line explosions with serious injuries is **anecdotal** (S116).

### D. Compliance and enforcement (C13)

1. **EPA RCRA penalty, December 2024 (S113).**
   - The Army **and American Ordnance LLC** agreed to pay **$39,311** "to resolve alleged violations" of the **2018 permit** in 2023:
     - improper detonation in a detonation chamber;
     - failure to properly close the relocated grenade detonation chamber;
     - **open burning in pans in excess of permit limits**.
   - Status: **settlement**. Admission status is unknown because the consent agreement/final order was not retrieved.
   - This is the only formal enforcement action found that names AO. It ties directly to the OB limits disputed in C1–C5.
2. **Older EPA formal actions.** FRS lists ICIS formal enforcement actions 07-1986-0192, 07-1990-0232, 07-1992-0262 and 07-2001-0114 (S114). Their content was not retrieved. The ECHO detailed facility report (violations, quarters in noncompliance) could not be accessed.
3. **Federal Facility Agreement.** The CERCLA FFA between EPA and the Army took effect on 1990-12-10. The 2018 permit **defers** corrective action for the listed SWMUs/AOCs to that FFA, and corrective measures in Section IX are "RESERVED" (S1 L4745–4800, L5607). A separate FFA for the former AEC sites was reported as drafted (S106).
4. **Iowa DNR.** NPDES major permit; wet-weather bypass reports in 2010, 2014 and 2015 (S115). No Iowa DNR administrative or consent order against IAAP or AO was found in the excerpts reviewed.

### E. Remedies and accountability: who pays (C13, C15)

1. **Cleanup is federally funded.**
   - The Army handles munitions-related contamination under DERP.
   - USACE handles AEC-legacy contamination under FUSRAP, with DOE Legacy Management taking long-term stewardship (S107).
   - A performance-based remediation contract (Tetra Tech) has been in place since 2004 (S105, S106).
2. The permit exempts the facility from RCRA financial assurance as a federal installation (S1 L9267, L9495; 40 CFR 264.140(c)).
3. **No court finding or judgment against AO** was found, at IAAP or elsewhere. The search was web-based only, and PACER was not searched.
4. **No EAB petition** on the 2018 permit was found (searched via web; the EAB docket was not directly queried).
5. **C15 (Response to Comments).**
   - EPA answered a groundwater/air comment by pointing to Superfund (S1 L83–93).
   - Its own permit text and the Superfund record document off-site groundwater migration and past private-well contamination (findings A1, A2, A8).
   - So the RtC was brief but not factually wrong: the Superfund program does carry that work.
   - The RtC did not mention the 2007 vintage of the risk assessment until comment #2, where it acknowledged the deferral itself.

### F. Emergency permit for lead azide detonation (S2): coordinator-requested section (C27–C29, C33, C34)

**F1. Event chronology (primary sources).**
- **Discovery and notification.**
  - EPA Region 7 was notified on **2026-03-12** of 68 drums of potentially unstable lead azide in two earth-covered magazines (S121).
  - Per court filings reported by CNN, the Army had convened a working group "as far back as January" 2026 (S128; underlying filing not read).
  - The drums had been stored "for over two decades" in a water/ethanol solution, and the lead azide recrystallized (S122).
- **Emergency storage permits.**
  - 1st: 2026-04-02 (S121).
  - 2nd: 2026-06-30 per S121, or 2026-07-01 per S120.
  - 3rd: 2026-09-29, effective to 2026-12-26. Its public notice was posted 2026-11-03, 35 days later (S118).
- **Community engagement before the treatment permit.** The Army held an open house on 2026-10-15 with about 200 attendees, with no date set for the detonation (S121, S139).
- **Treatment (detonation) permit S2.**
  - Effective 2026-11-13 through 2027-02-11.
  - EPA's notice page shows publish date **2026-11-16** (S119; also S122). EPA's letter says it was posted 11/13 (S121). This discrepancy is unresolved.
- **Army public notices.** The 5-day advisory went out 2026-11-15, the 48-hour notice 2026-11-18, and the detonation was postponed on 2026-11-19 (S121, S122).
- **Litigation.**
  - 2026-11-19: Iowa Environmental Council and Sierra Club petitioned for review (8th Cir. No. 26-3374).
  - 2026-11-20: the court entered an administrative stay.
  - 2026-11-21: EPA filed notice that the Army may proceed without the permit under the munitions emergency exclusion (40 CFR 270.1(c)(3)(i)(D)).
  - **2026-11-23: the court denied the emergency stay and lifted the administrative stay** (S122).
  - The petition for review itself remains **pending**.
- **Rescheduling.** The detonation was reset to about 9 a.m. CT on **2026-11-25** (S117, S134).

**F2. Has the detonation happened, and what were the results?**
As of the last searches on 2026-11-25:
- No source confirms that the detonation occurred.
- No post-detonation plume estimate, Army sampling result or EPA result was found.
- EPA said preliminary dust-wipe results would come "in the days following", with full QA later, and that results will be posted on its response site (S121).
- **Status: unknown, pending.** This must be re-checked.

**F3. Who bears the risk (C29).**
- S2 Condition IV.D does **not** say deposition is expected in Census Tracts 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 and 9. It requires "reasonable best efforts" to schedule the blast so as to **minimize** dust-lead deposition *within* those tracts (S2 p.9).
- Tracts 2–6 are predominantly the city of Burlington, and tracts 7 and 9 include parts of it (S131). Burlington had 23,982 people in 2020, out of 38,910 in the county (S132).
- So the named tracts are best read as the **populated areas the permit tries to protect**. C29's wording ("covers modeled lead deposition in ...") should be revised. Tract-level populations were not obtained.
- The Army's 2026-11-24 forecast put the plume **southwest**, away from Burlington and toward northeast Missouri (S130).
  - Its model showed **≥100 µg/ft² over about 27 sq mi** and **≥5 µg/ft² over about 148 sq mi** (S129).
  - A cellphone alert advised possible evacuation within 5 miles southwest and staying indoors up to 20 miles away (S130). The Army advised Middletown (population 363) and a 5-mile southwest cone to evacuate (S134).
  - The population inside the forecast cone has **not been established** and is primarily rural.
- **Schools.** Burlington, West Burlington, Burlington Notre Dame, Danville, Mediapolis, New London, Winfield-Mount Union and Columbus districts closed for the original 2026-11-20 date. Southeastern Community College altered classes (S133). Plans for 11-25 were not confirmed.
- **Sensitive groups.** S2 Appendix C names children, pregnant people and older adults (S2 p.23). The court record states the parties agree the detonation "will result in harm to the public through environmental and health risks" (S122). That is the court's description of the parties' positions, **not** a quantified harm finding.

**F4. Protectiveness of S2 thresholds against EPA lead benchmarks (C29, C33).**

| S2 parameter (S2 pp.16, 22, 24) | Benchmark (primary) | Comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Advisory Area at ≥5 µg/ft² modeled deposition: stay indoors, close windows, HVAC to recirculate | Dust-lead **action level** for floors = 5 µg/ft² from 2026-01-12 (40 CFR 745.227, S127). Dust-lead **hazard** = "any reportable level" on floors and sills (40 CFR 745.65(b), S126) | The advisory threshold equals the floor post-abatement action level. By EPA's current hazard definition, lower deposition can still be a hazard if it reaches indoor floors or sills. |
| Soil and wipe sampling within 24 h only where modeled deposition is ≥100 µg/ft², and only on parks, public facilities and private property with Rights of Entry | 100 µg/ft² = window-trough action level; sill action level 40; floor 5 (S127). Soil-lead hazard 400 ppm play areas / 1,200 ppm yard (S126) | The mandated sampling trigger is 20× the floor level and 2.5× the sill level. Areas modeled at 5–100 µg/ft² get advisories but no **permit-mandated** sampling. Properties without signed access get none. EPA's independent wipe sampling and Army pre/post soil sampling for consenting owners (S121) partly offset this. |
| Airborne outer contour 0.18 µg/m³ | Lead NAAQS 0.15 µg/m³ as a **3-month** arithmetic mean (40 CFR 50.16, S125) | The contour level is numerically above the NAAQS level, so air between 0.15 and 0.18 µg/m³ falls outside the mapped contour. The averaging periods differ: a few-hour plume at 0.18 µg/m³ would contribute far less than 0.15 to a 3-month mean. S2 gives **no stated health basis** for 0.18 µg/m³. Protectiveness cannot be judged from S2 alone. |
| Emission factor 1.00 | — | Conservative. EPA chose it over the Army's view that the release would be under 100% (S2 p.3). This cuts toward protectiveness. |
| "Reasonable best efforts" to schedule the blast and to notify the public (S2 pp.9, 23) | — | Soft obligations (C33). Hard limits do exist: the weather exclusions in IV.O.3, the timed notices in IV.G (5-day, 48-h, hourly from −6 h, +6 h) and the 30-day Final Impact Area Report (IV.Q.2). |

- **Scope caveat.** The TSCA dust standards apply to *indoor* floors, sills and troughs in housing and child-occupied facilities, based on wipe samples. S2's isopleths are *modeled outdoor deposition*. S2 borrows the same units and numbers, but the comparison is indicative, not a legal test.
- Whether the thresholds are protective depends on actual deposition and indoor infiltration, which are not yet known.

**F5. Public notice and engagement record.**
- **Facts.**
  - No draft permit or comment period was offered. EPA's notices say it is "neither requesting nor accepting comments" (S118, S119).
  - The 8th Circuit held that a 45-day comment period is not a prerequisite where it would be inconsistent with the emergency. It noted, without deciding, the APA military-function exception (S122). Status: **ruling on the stay motion only**.
  - The 3rd storage permit's notice was posted 35 days after issuance (S118).
  - Iowa HHS said it was "recently made aware" (S135).
  - Iowa City's manager declared that he learned of the blast from the news on Nov 17 (S117, a party declaration reported by news).
- **Allegations (petitioners, S123).** The petitioners allege that notice was inadequate, that the public lacked the information to evaluate the risk, and that alternatives were not scrutinized. The court found them not likely to succeed (S122).
- **Responses.**
  - Army open house (2026-10-15).
  - Timed advisories.
  - State resource page and State Emergency Operations Center activation (S116, S117).
  - EPA field team on site since 2026-11-16.
  - Point-of-use filters if public water systems exceed the lead action level.
  - An Army claims process for reimbursement (S121).

**F6. Was the lead azide stored without a permit beforehand? (C27, C34)**
- **What the record shows.**
  - S2 says the ECM storage area "is not covered" under the 2018 permit (S2 p.2).
  - EPA describes the first emergency permit as needed because the lead azide "was a new hazardous waste on their facility" (S121, 4/2/2026 entry).
  - This implies EPA treated the material as becoming a waste when its degradation was determined, around March 2026. Before then it was stored explosive stock, not hazardous waste.
  - On that reading, storage over the prior two decades was product storage outside RCRA permitting. The waste-storage period without a permit would be roughly 2026-03-12 to 2026-04-02, about three weeks, or longer if the January working group date (S128) marks an earlier waste determination.
- **Not established.**
  - When the material actually met the regulatory definition of waste (e.g., 40 CFR 266.202 for unused munitions and bulk explosives). This is a legal question; **qualified legal review is recommended**.
  - Whether generator accumulation provisions applied in the interim.
  - Whether surveillance should have detected the degradation earlier.
- **C34.** The S2 shield excludes "violations of RCRA that occurred prior to the effective date" (S2 p.4, II.B). So any pre-2026-11-13 violations remain enforceable. **No enforcement action concerning the lead azide was found.** EPA's own letter records the Army's view that leaving the drums in place "would also be out of compliance with the facility's RCRA permit, potentially resulting in regulatory enforcement actions" (S121).
- **Accountability gap to flag (C26).**
  - S2 names **only the Army** "as owner and operator" (S2 p.1). The 2018 permit names AO as operator (S1).
  - The public record reviewed does not say whether AO managed the two magazines, who performed stockpile surveillance, or whether AO bears any contractual responsibility.
  - **No finding against AO exists on this matter.**
- **Inconsistent quantity in reporting.** Reports give "nearly five tons", "nearly 10,000 pounds" and "6,900 pounds" (S117, S139, Stripes excerpt). S2 redacts the quantity. Refer to claim-auditor.

## 3. Contrary evidence

- **Indicators.** EPA lists Human Exposure and Groundwater Migration as **under control** (S101). ATSDR's 1999 overall category was "No Apparent Public Health Hazard" (S102). Documented private-well contamination was addressed by alternate water.
- **Federal accountability.** Cleanup is funded and structured under an FFA, DERP and FUSRAP. EEOICPA has paid hundreds of millions of dollars. These are substantial accountability mechanisms, even if their adequacy is debated.
- **Responsiveness to the 2018 comments.** The 2018 RtC did respond substantively to Iowa DNR by changing V.B.3.a (S1).
- **AO's record.** AO's documented penalties are modest: $39,311 jointly with the Army, and OSHA penalties of $5,877–$34,000. No court finding against AO was located.
- **S2 protections.**
  - S2 imposes an emission factor of 1.00 over the Army's objection.
  - It sets weather exclusions, timed notices, sampling and cleanup, and a 30-day impact report.
  - It preserves enforcement for prior violations.
  - The 8th Circuit found petitioners not likely to show the permit arbitrary, and framed the choice as controlled versus uncontrolled explosion (S122).
- **Who the permit protects.** The named census tracts are areas the permit tries to *protect*. They are not a predicted exposure zone, and the forecast plume pointed away from Burlington (S130).
- **Prior storage.** No source establishes unpermitted *hazardous-waste* storage before 2026. EPA framed the material as newly generated waste in 2026 (S121).

## 4. Confidence and limitations

**Confidence: moderate.**
- **High** for: the EPA Superfund profile facts, the 2024 EPA settlement, the S2 text, the 8th Circuit opinion, the EPA letter timeline, and the CFR benchmarks (all read in full).
- **Moderate** for: the ATSDR, OSHA, DOL and FRS details, which come from search excerpts or third-party mirrors.
- **Low** for: detonation outcome (unknown), tract populations (not obtained), and ECHO and Iowa DNR enforcement completeness.

**Limitations**
- ECHO, osha.gov, dol.gov, PACER and the EAB docket were not directly accessible. The 2021 FYR, the 2024 CAFO text, the full 2013 ATSDR LHC and the Army plume-map PDFs were not read.
- Absence of findings against AO reflects web searches on 2026-11-25 only.
- The S2 benchmark comparison mixes indoor wipe standards with outdoor modeled deposition. It is indicative only.
- The C27 analysis turns on when the material legally became a waste. That is a legal question; qualified legal review is recommended before any public statement.
- News items are recent and fast-moving. Re-verify after 2026-11-25.
