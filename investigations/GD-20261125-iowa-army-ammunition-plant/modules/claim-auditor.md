Prepared 2026-11-25 by the claim-auditor subagent. Scope: C1-C16, C19 (S1, the 2018 RCRA permit) and C20-C34 (S2, the Nov 2026 emergency permit), plus additions C35-C46 (flagged). This is labelled wherever it applies.

## 1. Question investigated

What material claims do the 2018 IAAP RCRA permit (S1) and the Nov 2026 lead-azide emergency permit (S2) make? Are they internally consistent? Does the public record support, contradict or fail to test them? Priorities set by the coordinator:
1. The OB/OD rate-limit inconsistency.
2. A whole-document sweep.
3. Superfund status, PFAS, groundwater and the 2007 risk assessment.
4. Permit status.
5. The S2 items: 44mm vs 40mm, UN0130, storage duration, lead thresholds, the wind-speed wording, public posting and news.

Conclusion: three different metrics are mixed (permit limit, design capacity, SOP/air limit). Only two contradictions are real:
- RTC "OD 60" vs permit OD 100;
- permit OB 140 vs a stated design capacity of 120.

The SOP's 70/60 are stricter operating limits that sit under the permit and are consistent with it. Under II.E.18.d the permit prevails where provisions conflict, so the binding OB ceiling is unclear: 140 (permit) vs 70/60 (incorporated SOP). The EPA 2024 release's allegation that OB exceeded "permit limits" in 2023 (S10) shows the OB limits matter in practice. Which number was exceeded is not public.

### 2.3 Whole-document sweep: other S1 issues (C39 detail; image-checked where marked)

- IV.J.2.a: non-closed containers to be "treated in the incinerator within 24 hours". No incinerator is permitted. The historical explosive waste incinerator is SWMU IAAP-025 (image p.52).
- IV.J.2.b: inspection "after the container is accepted at the Facility ... waste-receiving procedures". III.B.2 prohibits receiving off-site waste. This is boilerplate.
- IV.K.2 refers defect recording to IV.H.3 (incompatible waste) instead of IV.I (air emissions).
- VI.G.1.c (OD section): sample wells downgradient "of the Open Burn Area to verify that current open burning operations **are** contributing to groundwater contamination". Copied from V.H.1.c, which says "are not contaminating" (image p.67).
- VI.G.2: OD sampling report due "within 60 days of completing a response action". V.H.2 and VII.G.2 say within 60 days of sample collection.
- VII.G.5: response-action report due "within 60 days of the sample collection". V.H.5 and VI.G.5 say 60 days after completing the response action. The Section X summary table says "completion of a response action".
- VI.G.4.a-b omit groundwater from removal and verification. V.H.4.b and VII.G.4 include it.
- IX.K.1.a: corrective measures must comply with "threshold criteria identified Permit Condition IX.H.5.h.ii". Item ii is the balancing criteria; the threshold criteria are item i (image p.114).
- V.N, VI.M, VII.M, IX.V.5 cite 40 CFR 267.74. Part 267 is the standardized-permit rule; 264.74 is intended (image p.63). S2 N.2 repeats the same citation.
- V.G.1, VI.F.1, VII.F.1 cite "27 C.F.R. Part 55, Subpart K". ATF explosives storage is 27 CFR Part 555 Subpart K (image p.58). "DoD 4145.26M" is an outdated designation of the DoD contractor explosives safety manual; the text says "most recent version".
- VIII.I.4.a: statistically significant increase (SSI) tested only for VOCs and explosives. VIII.H.7.c defines SSI for explosives, VOCs and SVOCs.
- Section X summary cites "VIII.G.3.b" (should be VIII.H.3.b) and "IX.I.4 → X.U" (should be IX.U). V.H.1 is called an "SOP" in the summary but a "work plan" in the text.
- Waste codes: IV.B.1 lists D001, D003, D004-D009, D030, F001-F005, K044-K047. OCR showed "D009, D009"; the image shows D008 (image p.44). No error.
- Signatures and blank items: the permit is signed (C11). The RTC is unsigned (typical). Attachments VI-1 and VII-1 are blank placeholders (C36). PDF pp. 247-254 (post-closure photo pages) are dark photocopies with little legible content. PDF pp. 130 and 132 carry no OCR text (figure pages). No missing numbered permit pages were found: the "Page N of 124" headers are continuous where checked.
- OB secondary containment (V.B.2-3): a design is due in 24 months, but construction is required only "prior to resumption of routine open burning", "a different location" or "renewal application". The wording implies routine OB was not occurring in 2018. It is a deferred safeguard with an open-ended trigger.
- V.L.4 requires inspection for combustibles within 200 ft of each pan; the SOP requires clearing within 100 ft. These are different actions, so this is a mismatch but not a contradiction.
- Financial assurance: the closure plan (J-8 to J-12) claims a federal exemption from closure and post-closure financial assurance, even though a private LLC is co-permittee. The exemption in 264.140(c) covers the federal government. Whether it covers a contractor co-permittee was not tested (legal question; flag only).

**C29 comparison with federal benchmarks:**
- **Dust.** S2's 5 and 100 µg/ft² isopleths equal EPA's 2024 **dust-lead action levels** (DLAL) for **floors (5)** and **window troughs (100)**; the window-sill level is 40 (S25). Those are *indoor post-abatement clearance* benchmarks under TSCA lead-paint rules (effective Jan 2025, compliance Jan 2026). They are borrowed here for outdoor surfaces. The same 2024 rule set the dust-lead *reportable* (hazard) level at any reportable level above zero. S2 sets mandatory sampling at the least stringent DLAL (100, the trough value). Between 5 and 100 µg/ft² the permit requires only advisories, with no sampling. Sampling even at ≥100 is limited to parks, public facilities and private properties with Rights of Entry. Wet-cleaning instructions apply only at ≥100.
- **Air.** The lead NAAQS is **0.15 µg/m³ as a rolling 3-month average** (S26). The S2 0.18 µg/m³ outer contour is an hourly plume boundary, not a 3-month average, so the two are **not directly comparable**. As a rough illustration (inference): a 1-hour concentration of 0.18 adds about 0.00008 µg/m³ to a 3-month mean. The choice of 0.18 as the *outer* boundary means areas modelled below 0.18 µg/m³ in any hour are not shown on public maps at all.
- **Emission Factor 1.00.** This is a conservative modelling assumption imposed by EPA over the Army's view (S2 p.3, App. B). It is a protective feature.
- **Health statements.** Iowa HHS states "No safe level of lead exposure has been identified" (S37), and CDC has identified no safe blood lead level in children (as reported in S22). These are general statements, not predictions for this event.

**News and outcome:** The detonation was rescheduled from 20 Nov to 23 Nov and then to **25 Nov 2026, about 9:00 a.m. CST** (Army advisory, S27; EPA press release 24 Nov, S36). The evacuation zone is a 5-mile cone SW of the plant (S15). Reported quantity: 9,773.5 lb (DOJ figure via Earthjustice, S20). Other reports say about 9,500 lb or "nearly 5 tons". As of the final searches (2026-11-25), **no report confirming the detonation's outcome was found.**

## 3. Contrary evidence
- The C1-C5 "inconsistency" is mostly a metric mismatch (2.2). Only the RTC's OD 60 figure and the OB 140 > 120 design-capacity gap are true conflicts. The SOP limits are stricter and nest under the permit.
- C14: the PFAS omission predates the Army's 2020 PFAS preliminary assessment, so it is not evidence of concealment in 2018.
- C19: "IAAAP" is an official acronym, not a typo.
- C27: lead azide held as product before a waste determination generally needs no RCRA storage permit. The documented unpermitted-waste window may be short (Mar to 2 Apr 2026). EPA issued successive 90-day permits before each expiry (S17).
- C30: the "and" wording is almost certainly a drafting slip; enforcement would likely read "or".
- C25: the Army EA's water/ethanol storage supports a simple transcription error over a "dry/Forbidden" scenario.
- S2 has protective features: EPA imposed Emission Factor 1.00, hourly plume updates, post-event sampling, and closure and long-term remediation obligations.
- The 8th Circuit lifted its stay, citing the risk of an uncontrolled explosion without permit safeguards (S18). That is a procedural ruling, not a merits finding.
- The 2024 CAFO resolved *alleged* violations by consent. Nothing here is a court finding of liability.

- **Overall confidence: medium-high** for findings drawn directly from S1 and S2 (image-checked where they matter), and **medium** for public-record items.
- Several primary documents could be seen only as search-result excerpts because hosts were blocked or returned raw PDF bytes: iaaaprestoration.com (FYR addendum, RAB minutes, PFAS RI), yosemite.epa.gov (CAFO), response.epa.gov (EPA letter), CourtListener/Justia filings, SAM.gov. - The outcome of the 25 Nov 2026 detonation was not confirmed.
- Legal questions were flagged, not decided: the reach of the financial-assurance exemption, emergency-permit notice requirements, and whether the SOP or the permit governs OB rates.
