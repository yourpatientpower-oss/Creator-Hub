### 2.2 American Ordnance LLC: key dates (C16, C17)

- **Formed:** Formation and Operating Agreements dated **21 July 1998**, between General Dynamics and M&H. AO is a **Delaware LLC** (S911, S910). The state registry record itself was not checked.
- **IAAP operator since 1998**, succeeding M&H (1951–1998). AO is the successor-in-interest to M&H's Army contracts (S909).
- **Ownership:**
  - 50/50 GD and D&Z in Feb 2007 (S910).
  - D&Z says it took 100% in 2007 (S912).
  - AO's own Oct 2008 release says the joint venture "includes" ATK (S913).
  - D&Z in Sept 2026 calls it "a Day & Zimmermann company" (S914).
  - The 2007–2008 ownership sequence is **unresolved**.
- **Other plant:** Milan AAP, Tennessee, since 1998 (S915, S914).
- **Operating contract:** Oct 2008, with a 10-year base to ~2018 and up to 15 option years (S913). The base period ended at about the same time as the 2018 permit was issued; whether options were exercised was not checked.

### 2.3 Sequencing observations (observations, not findings about intent)

1. **SOP vintage (C7).** Condition V.F.3 incorporates OB SOP Rev. 6 (1 May 2000) and Change 2 (15 May 2006). The SOP attached as Attachment V-1 is Rev. 8 (21 Jan 2015), Change 2 (3 May 2018). The RTC says a revised OB SOP was accepted (S1 L120-124). So the permit text cites a version 15–18 years older than the one attached. The 1989 base date is the document's origin date, not its operative version.
2. **Old inputs.** The permit rests on a 2006 Part B, a 2003 Part A and a 1988 post-closure application. The 2007 risk assessment was 11 years old at issuance. The new risk assessment is conditioned on Army funding (C6), and the permit's own RA conditions are discretionary (C8). No later OB/OD risk assessment was found publicly (absence not proven).
3. **Limits vs practice.** OB has been permitted at 140 lb/hr since Sept 2018. Then:
   - an emergency permit allowed 3,000 lb/hr in Mar–May 2020 (S72);
   - EPA alleged burning above permit limits in 2023, settled in Dec 2024 (S73).
   - These dates fall before and around the missing TAR on OB alternatives (due ~Sept 2022).
4. **Lead azide storage (C27, coordinator items).**
   - 2004: DoD was concerned about a deteriorating 40-year-old IAAP lead azide stockpile (S94).
   - 2009: per news, the national stockpile was consolidated at IAAP (S90–S92).
   - 2018: the permit does not mention lead azide (OCR keyword search), and the storage magazines were outside permitted units (S2, S75).
   - Sept 2025: a reported neutralization requirement (S95, unverified).
   - Mar 2026: discovery.
   - 2026: three storage permits, then a treatment permit.
   - How long the material was stored is contested: "over two decades" (S79/DOJ) vs moved in 2009 (~17 years; S90–S92). Both could be true if pre-2009 IAAP material (cf. S94) was mixed with the 2009 transfers. Unresolved.
   - S2/C27's statement that storage was first authorized on 29 Sep 2026 conflicts with EPA's own notices (S74, S75), which call that the *third* emergency permit (first on 2 Apr 2026).
5. **Operator designation (C26).** The 2018 permit, the 2020 emergency permit and EPA's Dec 2024 release all name AO as operator. AO and D&Z still claimed the operator role on 21 Sep 2026, and AO received a shutdown-cost adjustment on 4 Sep 2026. S2 (13 Nov 2026) names the Army as sole "owner and operator". No source explains the change.
6. **Superfund deferral (C13).** The RTC's reliance on Superfund is consistent with the 1990 FFA. However, 36 years after listing:
   - the OU-4 final ROD was still in draft in 2025;
   - the OU-3 remedy had a performance issue in 2021;
   - PFAS investigations began 18 months *after* the permit was issued. This partly explains C14 by timing, though AFFF use predates the permit.
7. **Posting (C12).** The 2025-05 path matches the bulk posting of the whole Subpart X list (S70). That is a benign explanation for the posting date. It does not explain the "Draft" metadata title.

## 3. Contrary evidence

- EPA's RTC and permit openly disclose the 2007 RA date, the funding condition and the TAR schedule (C6, C9). These were not hidden.
- The 2020 increase was a lawful, time-limited emergency permit for propellant judged unsafe to store or transport (S72).
- The 2023 violations were resolved by a small settlement, with EPA stating compliance was restored (S73).
- The 8th Circuit found the Army evaluated "a full range" of alternatives and that EPA's reliance on that evaluation was reasonable (S79).
- The Army says lead azide was monitored monthly after 2009 (S91).
- The Rev. 6/Rev. 8 mismatch may be a drafting lag, not a substantive gap, since the RTC says the revised SOP was incorporated.

## 4. Confidence and limitations

**Confidence: medium-high** for the regulatory chronology from 2018 to 2026, which rests on EPA primaries S1, S2, S70, S72–S76 and S79. **Medium** for site history and AO ownership, which rest largely on search snippets, with several unresolved conflicts.

Limitations:
- The Wayback Machine was unavailable, so claim first-appearance dating was not done.
- The IAAP restoration site (FYRs, RODs) could not be opened directly.
- SAM.gov, state business registries and ECHO were not checked.
- The EPA letter to the Governor (S78) was unreadable.
- OCR noise affects several S1 dates (flagged above).
- Contract amounts are a sample, not totals (see money-mapper).
- Personal names in accident records were deliberately omitted.
