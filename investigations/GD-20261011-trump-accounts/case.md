## Neutral brief
Trump Accounts are a new type of tax-advantaged investment account for U.S. children. They were created by the 2025 reconciliation law and promoted heavily by the administration, with a federal seed deposit for children born in a set window and pledges from private donors and employers. Proponents present them as giving every child a financial stake in the economy. Critics have raised questions about who benefits most, given contribution-based design, about the role of financial firms that administer or invest the money, and about naming the program after a sitting president. This case documents how the accounts work, the stated and documented motives behind the push, and who benefits according to the available evidence. It does not presume wrongdoing.

## Scope
- In scope: statutory design and IRS/Treasury guidance; the official rollout and promotion; cost estimates (JCT/CBO); distributional analyses (e.g. Tax Policy Center, Urban, CBPP, AEI, Brookings, Penn Wharton); the selection and fees of custodians and asset managers; donor and corporate pledges (e.g. Dell) and their tax treatment; the legislative lineage (Invest America Act, baby bonds); lobbying and disclosures by financial firms; the stated rationales of officials; credible critiques and defenses.
- Out of scope: private individuals' accounts; personal data of account holders; general partisan commentary not tied to evidence; legal conclusions.

## Plan
- Questions to answer:
  1. Mechanics: eligibility, seed deposit, contribution limits, employer and philanthropic contributions, investment options and fee caps, withdrawal rules, tax treatment.
  2. Stated motives: what officials and sponsors said, and when.
  3. Documented incentives: which firms administer or invest the accounts and on what terms; lobbying for the provision; donor tax benefits; political and branding value.
  4. Distribution: who gains most by income or wealth group; take-up among lower-income families; comparison with alternatives (baby bonds, 529s, CTC).
  5. Fiscal cost and who bears it.
- Likely primary sources: statute text (congress.gov), IRS notices and forms (e.g. Form 4547), Treasury press releases, trumpaccounts.gov, JCT/CBO scores, Senate LDA lobbying disclosures, company press releases (e.g. Dell), White House statements, congressional hearing records.
- Modules to run: all 7. harm-accountability is reframed as "distributional impact, risks to participants, oversight and formal findings", because there is no allegation of consumer fraud.
- Evidence gaps (expected): actual take-up data; final custodian contract terms; any inspector-general or GAO review.
- Privacy / legal sensitivities: politically charged subject. Attribute every motive claim to a named source, and do not assert intent. Statements about named executives or donors must be limited to documented public acts.
