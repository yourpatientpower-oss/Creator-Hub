### 2b. Distribution (C7, C8)

1. **Defenders are clear on the mechanism.** Every independent analysis found agrees that the flat $1,000 seed is the same for every eligible child, and that balances beyond it scale with what family, employers and donors can deposit (up to $5,000 a year). Critics conclude that higher-income families gain most in dollar terms (HA-S1, HA-S14, HA-S15, HA-S16, HA-S17). Brookings notes that additional contributions are likely to go to 529s instead (HA-S12). AEI's right-of-center commentary also finds 529s superior for college saving (HA-S11).
2. **Important nuance for C8 (counter to a simple "tax break for the rich" reading).** By the JCT score, over 95% of federal cost ($14.6B of $15.2B, FY2025-34) is the flat $1,000 deposit, not tax preferences (HA-S19, not directly accessed; HA-S46). After-tax family contributions get only deferral, and earnings are taxed as ordinary income. Several analysts say this can be worse than a taxable brokerage account (HA-S46, HA-S14). So the federal outlay is distributed per eligible child, not by income. The skew to higher-income families comes mainly from private contributions (family, and employers, which favors corporate employees) and from unequal take-up. No quintile distribution table specific to Trump Accounts from TPC, JCT or CBO was found.
3. **Value of tax deferral by bracket.** Ghilarducci states that deferral "mainly benefits families in higher tax brackets" (HA-S14). The agent run surfaced two further items, neither verified by this analyst:
   - A Forbes analysis argues that pre-tax payroll contributions give larger savings to higher-bracket earners. Its worked example conflicts with the $2,500 employer cap (HA-S52).
   - Cato estimates that after-tax family money in a Trump Account trails a taxable account by ~$2,451 over 30 years (HA-S51).
   No verified quantification of deferral value by bracket was found. Evidence: gap.
4. **Take-up before auto-enrollment skewed away from lower-income families.**
   - Commonwealth found 5% among LMI parents (HA-S6).
   - Urban found an awareness gap by income and race (HA-S2) and modeled exclusion of non-filers (HA-S3, unverified).
   - AEI reported lower low-income participation (HA-S9).
   - Treasury's "86% under $200,000" (HA-S4) does not compare against the share of eligible families under $200k, so it cannot show relatively higher take-up among lower-income families (analyst inference about what the release omits).
5. **Response.** Treasury's 2026-10-01 auto-enrollment created accounts for 60M+ children (HA-S5). Urban and TPC analysts called it consistent with the evidence base, as a first step (HA-S7, HA-S18). The $1,000 still requires a claim plus a separate opt-in (HA-S5, HA-S43). No post-auto-enrollment claim data by income was found.
6. **Dell pledge targeting** ($250, children born 2016-2024 in ZIPs with median income <= $150k) is only weakly targeted: CNBC's analysis says ~3% of ZIP codes exceed that line (HA-S41).
7. **Defenders (C7).**
   - Treasury says the accounts "level the playing field" (HA-S40).
   - Sen. Cruz: "a generation of new capitalists" (HA-S34).
   - Brad Gerstner: "get all the people who have felt left out" (HA-S41).
   - Gov. Wes Moore (D) called them "actually a smart policy" (HA-S48).
   - Sen. Booker (D), sponsor of the baby-bonds bill, co-signed a letter with Cruz urging Fortune 1000 CEOs to contribute. He said the design is "not the version I would have done" but could be "game-changing" (HA-S49, secondhand via Roll Call).
   - Brookings: universal $1,000 grants "will reach some poor families" the CTC misses (HA-S12).
   - These are stated aims or partial findings. No independent projection was found showing the enacted design meaningfully reduces wealth inequality.
8. **Whole-law context, not attributable to Trump Accounts.** PWBM and Brookings find the overall OBBBA leaves the bottom 40% net worse off by 2030 or over a lifetime (HA-S12, HA-S13).

**C7 assessment:** a forecast that the available independent evidence does not support. It cannot be tested yet. The seed is universal, but most analysts expect balances to diverge by family means.
**C8 assessment:** supported for account balances and private-contribution benefits (consistent analyses, plus pre-October take-up data). Weaker for federal dollars, which are mostly a flat per-child deposit. Partly mitigated by auto-enrollment, whose effect is not yet measurable.

### 2c. Participant risks

- **Market risk.** Growth-period funds must be in broad U.S. equity index funds, so there is no bond or cash cushion (HA-S39). The default fund is the SPDR Portfolio S&P 500 ETF (HA-S17). The administration's own projections assume ~10% returns without inflation or tax adjustment; at 6.7% a $1,000 seed is ~$8k real after tax at 55 (HA-S10). New stock-donation rules add single-stock concentration risk, which Treasury acknowledged (HA-S9, HA-S43).
- **Fees.** There is a statutory 0.1% expense-ratio cap (HA-S39). Robinhood represented to SEC staff that it takes no account-level fees, while compensation flows through undisclosed Financial Agent agreements (HA-S28). No evidence was found of fees above the cap.
- **Tax and liquidity.** Earnings and seed money are taxed as ordinary income on withdrawal, with a 10% penalty for non-qualified uses before 59 1/2 (HA-S12, HA-S39). There are no hardship withdrawals before 18 (HA-S22). Basis tracking for after-tax contributions is a recordkeeping burden (HA-S22).
- **Scams.** Treasury issued its own warning (HA-S22, HA-S23). Secondary sites describe impostor activation texts and fee scams (HA-S24). No FTC or state AG action and no loss figures were found, so this is potential harm with a documented official warning.
- **Rollout problems.** Tax Notes documented problems as of late May 2026: a contradictory verification domain, a copycat app at launch, invalidated web-form elections, and a misstatement of qualified uses (HA-S21). Reddit posts show activation and processing failures, but this is a weak signal only (HA-S25). These are documented burdens. They are unquantified, and no formal finding addresses them.
- **Benefit interactions.**
  - SSA treats accounts as excluded for SSI during the growth period. Post-18 guidance is pending (HA-S26, HA-S27).
  - Other agencies (SNAP, TANF, Medicaid asset tests, FAFSA) had not issued guidance per CRS (HA-S26).
  - FAFSA treatment is disputed among commentators (HA-S27).
  - At conversion to a traditional IRA, SSI recipients face a possible resource-test cliff. The ABLE rollover window is narrow (year turning 17, whole balance) (HA-S26).
  - Per an agent-reported source, the balance counts for SSI from January 1 of the year the child turns 18. A CBPP calculation shows the $1,000 seed alone passes the $2,000 SSI limit by 18 even at 4% returns; the arithmetic checks (HA-S53). A bill to raise SSI asset limits (H.R. 2540) was introduced but has not been voted on (HA-S53).
  - 27% of LMI parents cite tax or benefit worries as a barrier (HA-S6).

### 2d. Oversight and formal findings

- **Formal findings: none found.** No GAO or TIGTA/OIG report, court ruling, regulatory order or enforcement action concerning Trump Accounts was found as of 2026-10-11. Absence in search is not proof of absence.
- **SEC staff no-action letter** (HA-S28). This is a staff enforcement position granting relief from Form CRS delivery. It is not a finding of compliance or wrongdoing. It confirms Robinhood's sole-broker role. It also says accounts cannot move to another broker-dealer while Robinhood is sole broker, while Quartz, citing WSJ, reports that rollovers will be allowed within a year (HA-S47). This is unresolved; see section 3.
- **Congressional oversight** is limited to questions:
  - The Warren-Garcia letter (HA-S31) asks whether the President directed March 2026 purchases of Robinhood and BNY stock before the 2026-04-06 designation. This is an allegation-framed inquiry, not a finding. The Trump Organization says trades are made by discretionary third-party managers. No response was found.
  - The Senate Finance exchange (HA-S32) produced no commitment to investigate.

### 2e. Naming and branding (C10)

- **Critics.**
  - Reps. Neguse and Scanlon at the House Rules Committee (HA-S33).
  - AEI's Viard, who says Republicans named the accounts "obsequiously" (HA-S10).
  - Rep. Bennie Thompson (HA-S17).
  - Hunter Schwarz, who argues the partisan name may deter opposite-party parents (HA-S36).
  - Tax Notes authors, who report philanthropist misgivings about "explicit partisanship of the branding" (HA-S21).
  - Gov. Wes Moore (D), who supports the policy: "I hate the fact that he called it Trump Accounts" (HA-S48).
- **Supporters.**
  - Sen. Cruz: precedent of Roth, Pell and Obamacare, and "doesn't matter what they're called" (HA-S34).
  - WH spokesman Kush Desai: "Only President Trump could've secured this historic win" (HA-S35).
  - Agent-reported, not verified: a House Ways and Means statement that the accounts provide security "just like their namesake" (HA-S50).
- **Neutralizing efforts.** Proponents including GOP operative Matt Lira, and analysts, push the "530A" label to depoliticize the accounts (HA-S35). Commonwealth itself uses "530A (Trump) Account" (HA-S6).
- **Documented effect relevant to C10.** 25% of LMI parents cited lack of trust in the Trump administration as a barrier (HA-S6). This shows a participation cost associated with the political association. It does not show the purpose of the naming.
- **Complaints and bills.** No ethics or Hatch Act complaint specific to the Trump Accounts name or promotion was found. Related but distinct items:
  - SERVE Act and Federal Property Integrity Act: introduced and referred, with no action found; whether they reach statutory accounts is unclear (HA-S37).
  - Public Citizen complaint (pending) and DNC lawsuit (pending) about taxpayer-funded Trump ads, not shown to involve Trump Accounts (HA-S44, HA-S38).
- **C10 assessment:** this is an opinion claim. Records document the rename and the attributed statements on both sides. They do not establish purpose. Do not state motive as a finding.

## 3. Contrary evidence

- The federal subsidy is overwhelmingly a flat per-child deposit (HA-S19/HA-S46), which tempers C8 as applied to taxpayer dollars.
- Auto-enrollment (HA-S5) removes the main access gap critics identified (HA-S3, HA-S6). Pre-October take-up data may overstate long-run skew.
- Brookings (generally center-left) credits the seed with reaching some children the CTC misses (HA-S12). A Democratic governor praised the policy (HA-S48), and Sen. Booker urged corporate contributions (HA-S49).
- Treasury's "86% under $200k" figure (HA-S4) is offered as evidence of broad reach. It is accurate as stated but lacks a denominator for comparison.
- Several risk sources are low-quality or secondary: scam sites (HA-S24), Reddit (HA-S25), and a watchdog site used as a pointer (HA-S20). The Tax Notes observations (HA-S21) are dated late May 2026 and may have been fixed since.
- Rollover portability is unresolved: the SEC letter says no transfer while Robinhood is sole broker (HA-S28), while Quartz, citing WSJ, reports rollovers allowed within a year (HA-S47). The first may describe the initial account only.

## 4. Confidence and limitations

- **Confidence:** medium.
  - High that no formal findings exist in the searched databases as of today.
  - Medium-high on the C8 mechanism (many independent sources agree).
  - Low-medium on quantified take-up by income (survey-based, pre-auto-enrollment).
- **Not directly accessed:** the JCT JCX-35-25 figures (HA-S19), the Urban January 2026 model (HA-S3, fetch failed), Treasury sb0508 (via press only) and the full CRS R48910. Forbes and POLITICO Pro were paywalled, so snippets only.
- **Gaps:** no Tax Policy Center or JCT quintile table for Trump Accounts specifically; no data on $1,000 claims by income after auto-enrollment; no quantified scam losses (no FTC Consumer Sentinel figure found); no CBPP piece specific to Trump Accounts surfaced in search; no Wayback archives were captured.
- **Agent run:** the Nimble Web Search Agent run (task_run_369852cfc63a4fbe82a20d8eafe85f43) completed after the first draft. Its items are in the ledger as HA-S50 to HA-S54 and are marked agent-reported where this analyst did not open them.
  - The agent conflated a Capital One story about Trump Organization bank accounts with the section 530A program. That item is excluded (HA-S55). The source auditor should watch for this name collision.
  - The agent's oversight list (TIGTA/GAO index pages, CREW Hatch Act complaints about other matters) contained nothing specific to Trump Accounts. That is consistent with the "none found" result.
- **Scope note:** the lawsuit *Trump v. IRS* (S.D. Fla., 2026) surfaced in the lawsuit search. It concerns tax-return leaks, not Trump Accounts, and is excluded.
