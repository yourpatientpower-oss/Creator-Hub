# Timeline Analyst: GD-20261011-trump-accounts

Author role: timeline-analyst (Wave 1). Run date: 2026-10-11. Sources are listed as `TL-S#` in `sources.md`. Claim IDs (C1–C12) come from `case.md`.

## 1. Question investigated

What happened, and in what order, from the precursor proposals (2023) to the present (2026-10-11)? That covers the creation, enactment, implementation, promotion, vendor selection, funding pledges, critique and oversight of Trump Accounts (IRC §530A, P.L. 119-21). It also asks where the order of events is worth flagging for other modules. Any sequencing notes below are **observations only**. None of them shows intent, causation or wrongdoing.

Method: Nimble Search, Extract and a Web Search Agent run. I also used the Wayback Machine CDX index and captures of trumpaccounts.gov, the Federal Register API and the Senate LDA lobbying API, all reached through Nimble Extract. Direct `curl` from the sandbox was blocked (proxy 403), and WebFetch could not resolve treasury.gov.

### 2.2 Sequencing observations (not findings of intent)

- **O1 – Industry design came first.** The corporate-match structure and contacts with Dell and Uber are documented by Nov 2023 (TL-S5). Dell is quoted in the bill's launch release (TL-S3). The Dells' $6.25B pledge, Notice 2025-68 and the website all went live on the same day, 2025-12-02 (TL-S27, TL-S29, TL-S30).
- **O2 – Late rename.** "Invest America" (S. 1718) became "MAGA accounts" in the House text, then "Trump Accounts" through a Rules Committee amendment on the evening before the House vote (TL-S2, TL-S8, TL-S9).
- **O3 – A "no cost" claim alongside a scored cost.** The President's 2025-06-09 statement that the program costs taxpayers nothing (TL-S18) sits next to JCT scores of about $17B (House bill) and about $15B (enacted) (TL-S12, TL-S13). For claim-auditor.
- **O4 – Selected vendors already in the coalition.** Robinhood's CEO attended the 2025-06-09 roundtable (TL-S20). BNY and Robinhood were on Treasury's pledge list on 2026-01-29 (TL-S38). Both were selected on 2026-04-06 (TL-S40). ICI had argued against a single provider in Oct 2025 (TL-S25). S. 1718 had proposed fee- and performance-based selection (TL-S2). How the 2026 selection was made is not documented in the sources reviewed.
- **O5 – State Street.** It appears on the pledge list (2026-01-29), registered lobbyists for "TRUMP Accounts" on 2026-06-05, and was named the default fund manager on 2026-07-01 (TL-S38, TL-S63, TL-S48). SPYM's low cost is a plausible benign explanation. The timing alone does not show influence.
- **O6 – Reported presidential trades.** Secondary summaries of the President's OGE disclosures report purchases of BNY and Robinhood stock in Q1 2026, before the April 6 selection, and of Dell stock on 2026-02-10, after the Dell pledge and before the July 6 "buy Dell" remark (TL-S45, TL-S46, TL-S54). Bessent attributes the trades to an outside manager. No regulator has made a finding. The primary 278-T/278e filing has not yet been checked.
- **O7 – Website claim changed.** From 2025-12-02 to 12-15 the site credited the $1,000 to "Donald J. Trump". By 2025-12-17 it credited "the U.S. Treasury" (TL-S31–S33). From at least 2025-12-17 to 2026-01-01 the site showed an individual-stock portfolio mock-up. It was gone by 2026-10-01 (TL-S33–S35).
- **O8 – Automatic enrollment came by temporary rule.** In Dec 2025, Treasury indicated it did not plan to auto-enroll "at least initially" (TL-S27). On 2026-09-30, temporary regulations took effect on publication. Enrollment of 60M+ children was announced complete on 2026-10-01, and the White House event followed on 10-07. The comment period on the companion proposed rule runs to 2026-11-30, after the enrollment and after the 2026-11-03 election (TL-S39, TL-S61, TL-S62).
- **O9 – Projections drifted.** For $1,000 with no contributions: "nearly $674,000" by retirement (2025-12-17, TL-S36), then "at least half a million" (2026-01-28, TL-S37), against the CEA's $5,800 at age 18 (TL-S24).
- **O10 – Income-mix figures differ.** Bessent gave 86% under $200k on 2026-07-06 (TL-S54). The White House gave "an estimated 80%" on 2026-10-07, after auto-enrollment (TL-S62). Neither is a distributional analysis.
- **O11 – Stated rationale evolved.** Financial literacy, then "a backdoor for privatizing Social Security" (walked back the same day; 2025-07-30), then "every American a shareholder" and "not a government program" (2025-12-17), then "triumph of capitalism over socialism" (2026-01-28) (TL-S22, TL-S36, TL-S37).

## 3. Contrary evidence

- **Bipartisan lineage.** Child-account proposals predate the GOP version (Booker/Pressley, TL-S4), and Gerstner pitched the idea as cross-partisan (TL-S5, TL-S6). This weakens a reading in which the idea originated purely as a political project.
- **Statutory protections.** Investments must be broad index funds with fees capped at 0.10% (TL-S27). The default fund and the four alternatives are low-cost ETFs from three managers: State Street, BlackRock (iShares) and Vanguard (TL-S48). The app is white-labelled, as ICI recommended to blunt a single provider's advantage (TL-S25, TL-S40). Rollovers to any qualified trustee are allowed (TL-S43).
- **Breadth of lobbying.** The LDA filings show many firms lobbying, including credit unions seeking inclusion (TL-S63). No BNY or Robinhood filing containing the exact phrase "Trump Accounts" was found. Lobbying by the selected fund manager is one of many such registrations.
- **Access and targeting.** Automatic enrollment addresses the take-up gap Urban reported for lower-income families (TL-S56, TL-S61). The Dell pledge is targeted at lower-income ZIP codes (TL-S27). The White House says most accounts belong to families earning under $200k (TL-S62).
- **Correction.** The "courtesy of Donald J. Trump" wording was replaced within about two weeks (TL-S33).
- **Trades.** The reported trades are unverified at primary level. Bessent attributes them to an outside manager (TL-S45). No formal finding exists.

## 4. Confidence and limitations

**Overall confidence: Medium–High** for the dates of official actions (statute, Federal Register, Treasury and White House releases, Wayback captures, LDA filings). **Low–Medium** for the reported presidential trades.

- Sandbox network calls (`curl`) were blocked by proxy policy (403), and WebFetch could not resolve treasury.gov. All primary pages were retrieved through Nimble.
- Several URLs were found only by the Nimble Web Search Agent and were not opened by me: TL-S1 (P.L. text), TL-S4, TL-S15, TL-S16, TL-S42 and TL-S66. They are marked as such in `sources.md`. The agent's output also had errors that I corrected against primary sources: it dated the summit to February 2026, and its lobbying claims were attributed to a source that would not support them.
- The OGE 278-T/278e filings were not reviewed. The trade items rest on a senator's press release and a syndicated Quiver/Barchart summary.
- The LDA review covered only the exact phrases "Trump Accounts" (2025–2026) and "Invest America Act" (2025). Filings using other wording (for example "child investment accounts" or "530A" alone) may have been missed. Q3 2026 reports are not due until 2026-10-20.
- No Treasury–BNY financial agency agreement, solicitation or fee terms were found. The selection date is known, but the process is not.
- The Wayback analysis covered only the trumpaccounts.gov homepage. No new archives were created. For the 2026-10 capture's projection widget, I could not tell which age tab the displayed $15,000 figure refers to.
- Two dates are inferred from weekday references in TL-S50: 07-01 and 07-02-2026.
- No GAO, Treasury OIG or TIGTA product specifically on Trump Accounts was found (the agent's GAO and OIG hits were generic index pages, so they are not cited).
