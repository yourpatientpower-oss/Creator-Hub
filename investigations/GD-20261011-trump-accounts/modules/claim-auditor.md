## 1. Question investigated

What do the statute, IRS/Treasury guidance and JCT say about how Trump Accounts work (C1-C4, C11, C12)? What exactly have officials and sponsors said about why the accounts exist (C5)? Has the Dell pledge been made and paid (C6)? Does independent evidence support the forecast that the accounts will meaningfully reduce wealth inequality or help low-income children (C7)? Which other material claims in the program's own materials need testing (C13-C16)?

### Key corrections to case.md paraphrases
- **C1:** The seed is elective (sec. 6434 election), not automatic. The window is births 2025-01-01 to 2028-12-31. No deposits before 2026-07-04.
- **C3:** Employer money (max $2,500 per employee, not per child) counts **within** the $5,000 annual cap.
- **C4:** Seed, philanthropic and employer money is fully taxable on withdrawal, not just the earnings. Access begins Jan 1 of the year of turning 18.
- **C6:** The final Dell threshold was ZIP median family income <=$117,300, not $150,000. It covered children born 2016-2024. Deposits were reported complete on 2026-10-09 (non-independent source).
- **C11:** The exact figure is $15.233B (FY2025-34, JCX-35-25), about 96% of it seed outlays in FY2025-28.

### Current program facts (as of 2026-10-11)
Contributions opened 2026-07-04 (CA-S4, CA-S9). The default investment is SPYM (CA-S7). Temporary regulations effective 2026-09-30 created automatic accounts for children with SSNs. The March 2026 proposal was withdrawn, and comments on the new proposal are due 2026-11-29 (CA-S16, CA-S17). Sec. 128 employer regulations are still proposed (CA-S17).

## 3. Contrary evidence

- **Against critics (C7):** CRS confirms that the flat seed is progressive in percentage terms (CA-S18). Auto-enrollment, which critics demanded (CA-S22), was adopted (CA-S16). The Dell money went to lower-income ZIPs first (CA-S19). Much of the critical analysis (CA-S21, CA-S22, CA-S25) predates auto-enrollment or analyzed the House bill, so its take-up and tax-treatment premises are partly outdated.
- **Against officials (C13-C15):** growth figures are nominal and pre-tax, and official sources disagree with one another. Enrollment-by-income shares have no published method. "Automatic $1,000" conflicts with the statute.
- **Source independence:** C5 and C13-C16 rest mainly on statements by the subject (Treasury, White House, trumpaccounts.gov), which share an administration origin. Dell payment completion rests on Invest America, the donor's partner. Independent corroboration exists for the statute and JCT (high) and for CRS analysis (high), but not for deposit totals or income shares.
- **Unverified agent leads, not used as evidence:** Bessent's 2026-01-28 speech (SB0372); Smith statement of 2026-09-30; Brookings, Aspen, EPI and WashU CSD pieces; BNY/Robinhood as trustee (owned by money-mapper). These were reported by the Nimble agent but not opened in this session.

## 4. Confidence and limitations

- **Overall confidence: medium-high.** High for mechanics (C1-C4, C11), which are read directly from statute, IRS and JCT text. Medium for C6, C7, C12 and C15, which depend on non-independent sources, interpretive commentary or missing data.
- Full text of the 2026-09-30 temporary regulations was not read; only Federal Register metadata and a Thomson Reuters summary were. Rules on auto accounts, approved classes and stock gifts may have nuances not captured.
- Several think-tank items (CA-S21 to CA-S23) were read as search-result excerpts, not full pages. CA-S21 analyzed the House version.
- No independent microsimulation of distribution by income (TPC, PWBM, CBO) was found. This is the main gap for C7.
- The comparison of seed deposits with JCT's FY2026 outlay is an inference with timing differences: JCT assumed FY2025 outlays that could not occur before 2026-07-04.
- Wayback availability returned no snapshots for trumpaccounts.gov or the Invest America Dell page. Both pages are live and changeable.
- No legal conclusion is drawn. "Contradicted" for C14 refers to the accuracy of a public statement against the statute, not to intent.

## 5. Next best research action

Obtain Treasury or IRS administrative data, through Treasury, a FOIA request or the full T.D. 10056 preamble and regulatory impact analysis, on seed elections and payments, claimed versus unclaimed auto accounts, and contributions by family-income band. Then pair these with an independent distributional estimate (TPC, PWBM or CBO, if any exists). This single dataset would test C7, C15 and C16 and settle the seed take-up gap implied by $1.3B paid versus JCT's $6.6B FY2026 assumption.
