Prepared 2026-11-20. Claims covered: C4, C6, C7, C8, C14, C16 (plus context for C5, C10, C11).
Tools: Nimble Search and Extract. ## 1. Question investigated
What harm, remedies, oversight actions and formal findings are documented for each strand, and what status does each item have?
- (a) The Italian-unit (Deutsche Bank S.p.A.) losses.
- (b) Epstein and Deutsche Bank.
- (c) Trump and Deutsche Bank: the 2017 tax-document error and the SARs.
- (d) Official responses to the "Inside Deutsche Bank" leak.
- (e) Paolo Zampolli: formal proceedings only, and only those relevant to Epstein.

## 2. Findings

### 2a. Italian unit (C4, C5, C6, C7)
**Formal findings: none located.** No sanction, order, restatement, prosecution or public statement from the ECB, Banca d'Italia, BaFin or Consob about the mortgage-loss matter was found as of 2026-11-20.
- The ECB declined to comment on substance (S110). ECB Vice-President Vujčić said "we do not comment on individual cases" (S109).
- BaFin said it will act if there are relevant indications beyond facts already known (S110). A search-engine summary claimed BaFin was "in contact with DB and the ECB and assessing the facts". No underlying source text supported that, so it is **not recorded**.

**Oversight that did happen.** All of the following comes from the subject or from leak-based reporting:
- DB says it reported the matter to the competent supervisors and the auditor (S3, S107).
- FT says DB told the ECB in 2020 of a €522m hit and a €779m potential loss (S106, S5). Il Fatto breaks this down as €522m crystallised plus €257m mark-to-market, plus €18m extra tax (S108).
- The external auditor (KPMG, per S106 and S108) concluded that no restatement of DB S.p.A.'s prior accounts was needed. This rests on DB's statement (S107), and FT reports it (S106).
- FT also reports that the auditor had missed the "tesoretto" during the period (S106). An unnamed source told the FT that regulators were "absolutely furious" (S106). That is anonymous and unverified.

**Documented harm (who bore the loss).**
- *DB AG, and so its shareholders:* about €1bn of capital was injected into the Italian unit in 2018-2023, according to the 2023 annual accounts as reported by FT (S106, S108). The reporting places this next to the losses but does not, in the excerpts seen, attribute the whole €1bn to them. DB also waived about €235m of underpaid internal funding costs (S106).
- *Italian mortgage customers:* **no documented customer harm.** The reported mechanism is customers using their legal right (the 2007 Bersani law) to repay or refinance early, which turned the bank's pricing and hedging assumptions into losses (S106, S108).
- Il Fatto says CEO Sewing found no negative impact on clients (S108). His sourced Reuters remarks are about client *reaction to the leak* (S118), so this should not be read as a finding on mortgage customers.

**Responses (internal accountability, all leak-based).**
- Five staff received a collective written warning. The three most senior were cleared of personal misconduct but reprimanded over management shortcomings (S106).
- No one was dismissed. €5.25m of deferred pay was not clawed back (S106, S108). Departing Italian top management received €8.1m of severance in 2020 (S108).
- DB's position is that corrections and accountability measures were taken (S3, S107).

**Disclosure question (no legal conclusion).**
- DB says no group-level disclosure was needed because the matter was not a material driver of group or segment performance (S107).
- The only sourced critique located is from a retired accounting professor quoted by the FT. He said the conduct was at odds with basic accounting rules and produced misstatements (S106).
- I found **no source** that applies a specific disclosure rule (MAR Art. 17, IFRS/IAS 8, the Italian TUF or Consob) to this matter. A search-engine summary implied that experts had raised MAR, but no source text supported it, so it is not recorded.
- Whether any rule was engaged is unresolved and needs qualified review.

**Historical context (separate matters, not about these losses).**
- Banca d'Italia fined DB S.p.A. board members €210k in 2012 over internal-control failings (S108, secondary).
- Consob fined DB €100k in 2020 over controls on the Finanza & Futuro adviser network (S130).

### 2b. Epstein (C8)
- **NYDFS consent order, 6-7 July 2020 (regulatory order).** The Department *found* that DB conducted business in an unsafe and unsound manner (NY Banking Law §44) and failed to maintain an effective AML program (3 NYCRR §116.2). Penalty: $150m. It credited DB's "exemplary cooperation" and remediation (S101, S131). I found no admit/deny clause in the text I searched. The bank consented to the order.
- **Jane Doe v. Deutsche Bank, SDNY (settlement without admission).** $75m class settlement, final approval by Judge Rakoff on 2023-10-20. The bank did not acknowledge wrongdoing (S102). This is the clearest *quantified remedy* to victims.
- **Wyden report, 4 Aug 2026 (partisan minority staff report; allegations and recommendations, not findings).**
  - It says DB retroactively flagged more than $250m of Epstein wires after his 2019 arrest, and that Epstein banked at DB for almost six years "without a real-time SAR filing in compliance with federal law".
  - It states there has been "no enforcement action at the federal level" against DB over Epstein.
  - It recommends Treasury, Fed and OCC investigations (S103, S104).
  - DB said it regrets the historical connection and has cooperated with authorities (S105).
- **Leak figure.** The leak reports $581.9m across 21 SARs (about 3,000 transactions, 2013-2019) (S128, S1, S2). That is a count of *flagged* transactions, not a finding of illegality. "DB has not been charged" (S128).
- **New regulator response to the $582m figure: none located.** The whistleblowers gave the data to DOJ and FinCEN (and HMRC per S116) (S115, S116). Receiving data is not opening a case. "Could face" probes (S116) is potential only.

### 2c. Trump (C10, C11)
- **No formal finding located** on the March 2017 tax-document mis-send or on the SARs about Trump-linked entities. Sources are leak reporting (S1, S2, S6).
- Trump publicly called the disclosure "illegal" (S117). That is an assertion by an affected party, not a finding, and I found no complaint filed.
- *Context, not about DB conduct:* in the NY AG civil fraud case, DB was a lender, not a party. The Appellate Division (Aug 2025) upheld Trump's liability for fraudulent financial statements to lenders and insurers but vacated the roughly $500m disgorgement. The NY AG said it would appeal (S119). I did not check the Court of Appeals outcome.

### 2d. Leak: government and regulator responses (C16)
- **German Finance Minister Klingbeil (statement).** He called for "full transparency" and said supervisors and, where criminally relevant, prosecutors would evaluate the leak (S111, S110).
- **Hesse data-protection commissioner (pending investigation, opened 18 Nov 2026).** He is examining DB's technical and organisational safeguards, the timeliness of breach notification and the notification of affected people. He has requested documents and declined to discuss sanctions (S112, S113).
  - The GDPR fine of up to €10m or 2% of turnover comes from an outside expert's opinion and is a **potential** harm only (S114).
- **BaFin:** conditional ("will act if new indications"). **ECB:** no comment (S109, S110).
- **Prosecutors:** no Frankfurt or German prosecutor probe and no criminal complaint about the data theft located. US: data received by DOJ and FinCEN (S115), and no announced action.
- **DB response (C16):** the matters are historical and DB is "a different bank" (S111, S118). The €7bn controls figure rests on S1 only, so I did not re-verify it.
- **Market (documented, quantified):** shares fell up to 6.2% intraday on 17 Nov (S129). This is temporary and is not a loss finding.

### 2e. Zampolli (C14)
- The **House Judiciary Committee Democratic staff report** (26 Oct 2026) is a partisan staff report and **not a finding**. It concludes that Trump and Zampolli "may have been co-conspirators" and calls for a special counsel (S120, S121).
  - Zampolli denies it: "Jeffrey Epstein was my competitor and rival ... never had any business dealings" (S122).
  - The White House says "None of these allegations are true" (S123).
  - A House Oversight Republican report (10 Nov) says Trump was "not implicated" (S124). That is also a partisan report.
  - A DOJ rebuttal was located only for a separate Oversight Democrats letter, not the HJC report (S125). The "DOJ rebuttal" of the HJC report itself remains unverified.
- **No criminal charge, indictment or court finding against Zampolli relating to Epstein was located.**
- One pending civil suit (SDNY 1:26-cv-07582, filed 2026-09-03) concerns an alleged 2023 incident. On its face it is not Epstein- or DB-related, it has not been adjudicated, and it is out of scope (S127).
- **No source links Zampolli to Deutsche Bank, the leak or the Italian unit** (search run 2026-11-20).

## 3. Contrary evidence
- DB's position: the matter was historical and was reported to supervisors and the auditor, the auditor found no restatement needed, and it was immaterial at group level (S3, S107). No regulator has contradicted this publicly.
- The losses arose from customers lawfully refinancing. That cuts against customer harm (S106, S108).
- The NYDFS order credits DB's "exemplary cooperation" (S101). The $75m settlement carried no admission (S102).
- The Wyden and HJC reports are partisan minority products. The Oversight GOP report reaches the opposite view on Trump (S124). Zampolli and the White House deny the allegations (S122, S123).
- Reuters could not independently verify the leak reports (S117).

## 4. Confidence and limitations
**Confidence: medium.** It is high for the 2020 NYDFS order, the 2023 settlement, the Wyden report text and the Hesse probe. It is medium-low for the Italian-unit figures, which are leak-derived, came through FT excerpts only and have no regulator confirmation.

Limitations:
- No Web Search Agent run was possible.
- FT is paywalled, and the HJC PDF was read in excerpts only.
- I did not search the Banca d'Italia sanctions register or Consob bulletins directly.
- No Wayback copies were made.
- "Not located" means not found in these searches. It is not proof of absence.
- One ID collision occurred in sources.md: my NYDFS press-release row was renumbered to S131.
