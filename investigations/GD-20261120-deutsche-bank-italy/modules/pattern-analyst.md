# Pattern Analyst — GD-20261120-deutsche-bank-italy

Module owner: pattern-analyst (Wave 2). Prepared 2026-11-20. Inputs: `case.md` (C1-C23), `sources.md`, and the four Wave 1 modules (claim-auditor, money-mapper, timeline-analyst, harm-accountability-analyst).

**Method and tools.** I built candidate patterns from the Wave 1 findings and then ran 8 targeted Nimble Search queries. Each query was meant to confirm or refute one specific instance or comparator: SEC 2015, MPS/Santorini, the 28,000 clients figure, Fed 2023, NYDFS/FCA 2017, JPMorgan/Epstein, the FinCEN Files response, and a re-check for any recent "Zampolli" + "Deutsche Bank" reporting. All new material was seen as search-result text only. No full pages were fetched. New sources are S150-S161.

**Rules applied.**
- A pattern needs at least two instances from **independent origins**. Several instances that trace to one leak origin (O-FT-ITALY / O-FT-LEAK / O-LEAK-CONSORTIUM) count as one origin when judging independence.
- A single instance is labelled an *observation*.
- Patterns describe mechanics only. No pattern below is evidence of intent, and none is a legal conclusion.

## 1. Question investigated

Do tactics or mechanics repeat across the documented events in this case? The events are the DB S.p.A. loss matter, Epstein, Trump, the "Inside Deutsche Bank" leak, and earlier DB matters (SEC 2015, MPS 2009-2022, NYDFS/FCA 2017, NYDFS 2020, Fed 2023).

The four candidate patterns come from the coordinator:
- (a) "historical / reported to supervisors / not material" responses;
- (b) profit or loss pressure met by accounting devices;
- (c) weak individual accountability;
- (d) high-risk clients retained despite warnings.

As a separate question: does the evidence support **any** pattern linking Paolo Zampolli to Deutsche Bank?

### 2b. Narrative findings

1. **The best-documented recurring mechanic is P4**: AML and client-risk controls were found deficient, and remediation lagged after earlier orders. This rests on independent formal findings: FCA/NYDFS 2017 (S161), NYDFS 2020 (S101), and the Fed 2023 finding of "insufficient remedial progress" against the 2015/2017 orders (S157). The leak's Epstein and 28,000-client material (S86, S154, S156) is consistent with that record but adds no independent verification. The Trump/Baku item should **not** be counted: by the memo's own account the companies were not DB customers (S53).
2. **P2 (losses kept out of reported numbers) is moderately supported.** It rests on one formal SEC finding (2015, S150) and the leak-reported Italian tesoretto. The two are similar in kind but not identical in mechanism. **MPS is a counterexample, not an instance.** DB was acquitted on appeal and in Cassation, and the alleged concealment concerned MPS's accounts (S152, S153). The "profit-target pressure" element (P2b) is documented only for Italy.
3. **P1 is real and also ordinary.** DB's "historical / controls invested" framing recurs from 2020 to 2026 (S159, S158, S105, S18, S107), and peers use the same language (HSBC, JPMorgan; S159, S160). It shows consistent messaging. It does not show non-disclosure. The narrower "supervisor told, public not" mechanic (P1b) is weak: its instances are leak-derived and mechanically different, and SAR confidentiality is a legal requirement.
4. **Accountability (P3):** "no admission" settlements recur, but they are industry-standard (P3a). The specific Italian mechanic (no dismissals, severance, no clawback) is **one instance**. Individual outcomes in the other DB matters were not sourced by any module. That is a gap, not a pattern.
5. **Zampolli (P5): the evidence supports no pattern and no single documented link.** The apparent connection comes from three co-occurrences: news-cycle timing, Milan geography, and each party's separate association with Epstein or Trump. None of these documents a relationship with Deutsche Bank. This conclusion covers published reporting only. The leak corpus and Italian lien registers are not public and were not examined.

## 3. Contrary evidence

- **Base rates.** Large banks routinely face AML orders and settle without admission, and peers use the same "historical" framing (S159, S160, S103). Patterns P1 and P3a are therefore not distinctive to DB. Even P4's distinctiveness rests on repeat orders on the *same* subject (S157), and I did not benchmark that against peers.
- **MPS acquittal** (S152, S153). The only prior *Italian* concealment allegation involving DB failed in court.
- **No intent finding** (C19). DB's own Italian probe reportedly found competence failures, not fraud (S10, S15). The SEC 2015 order was settled without admission (S151) and called valuation "complex".
- **Remediation claims.** NYDFS credited "exemplary cooperation" (S101). DB cites €7bn in controls (S18). The Fed in 2023 noted "some progress" (S158).
- **Independence limit.** Many instances (the Italy items, the 28,000 clients, the US swap, the Trump mis-send) trace to one leak origin and have not been independently verified (S154, S156, S106).

## 4. Confidence and limitations

- **Confidence: medium overall.**
  - High that P4 is documented by formal findings and that P5 has zero documented instances in public reporting.
  - Medium for P2. Low for P1b and P3b, which are single-origin or single-instance.
- **Access.** All new sources (S150-S161) were seen as search-result text only. I read no full orders: not SEC 2015, Fed 2023, FCA 2017, or the Cassation ruling. The date of the Cassation ruling was not captured. The FT "Katastrophe" piece (S154) is a gift link seen via snippet.
- **Unexamined.** Individual-accountability outcomes for Epstein, SEC 2015 and Fed 2023. Peer frequency of repeat AML orders. Whether Italian variable pay was linked to the targets.
- **Scope.** Patterns describe recurring mechanics in the documented record. They do not show that any particular person or unit acted with intent, and they are not legal findings. Public-facing use should have qualified legal review.
