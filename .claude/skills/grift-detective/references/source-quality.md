# Source independence and quality

Evaluate each source on: relevance, proximity to the original evidence, method transparency, expertise, corroboration, recency where needed, and conflicts of interest.

- A primary source is not automatically truthful. It is often the best source for what its author actually stated or filed. Test substantive claims independently.
- A citation count is not corroboration. Multiple articles repeating one press release share one **Origin ID** and count as one.
- Prefer sources independent of the subject, with no commercial tie, affiliate payment or sponsorship.
- Check dates. A true statement from 2019 may not describe 2026.
- Check scope. Apparent conflicts often answer different questions (different entity, period, jurisdiction).
- Resolve contradictions by source quality, independence, chronology and scope. Keep unresolved disagreements visible. Do not smooth them over.

## Source audit checklist (per major conclusion)
1. A source exists.
2. It supports the exact statement, not a looser one.
3. Date and context are confirmed.
4. Primary or secondary is noted.
5. Independent corroboration is noted, or its absence.
6. Paywalls, deleted material and inaccessible pages are noted.
7. Unsupported claims are removed.
