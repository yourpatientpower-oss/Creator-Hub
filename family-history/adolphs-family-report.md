# The Adolphs Family: Lippe, Germany → Iowa, USA

Research notes on the maternal line of an adopted father: Ruby Myrtle Adolphs (Smith), her father Ernest F. Adolphs, and her grandfather Friedrich (Frederick) J. Adolphs.

Compiled 1 November 2026 from public sources. Every fact below names its source. Items marked **(unverified)** came from one secondary source only, or could not be opened directly to check. Social Security numbers that appear in the public indexes are deliberately left out of this report.

## Generation 1: Friedrich Johann Jobst Adolph(s), alias Fritzemeier (1831–1896)

- **Born** 1 May 1831 at Bavenhausen No. 24, Lippe-Detmold; christened 8 May 1831 at Talle.
- **Parents:** Hans Hermann Adolph and Sophie Adolph. These match "Hans H Adolph" and "Sophie Adolph" in the Ancestry tree.
- **Occupation:** *Schweizer* (professional dairyman/cheesemaker) at **Gut Mönchshof**, a *Rittergut* (noble estate) near Barntrup. The estate belonged to the von Kerssenbrock family. Its manor house was built in 1764.
- **Alias "Fritzemeier":** in Lippe a person was often known by a farm or house name as well as the family name. US records may use either.
- **Marriages** (per Find a Grave biography; unverified beyond that):
  1. Dorothea Wilhelmine Heuer (1821–1863)
  2. **"Dora" Dorothea Elisabeth Carolina Stammeier (1841–1878)**, daughter of Henrich Stammeier and Luise Ridderbusch (per the Ancestry tree)
  3. Clementine Wilhelmine Charlotte Schafmeister (1848–1880)
- **Emigrated** to the USA in **1892**, at about age 61.
- **Died** 1896; **buried Flood Creek Cemetery**, Ulster Township, Floyd County, Iowa (Find a Grave memorial 232053819).
  - *Conflict:* the same memorial's biography says he "returned to Germany after three years." The Iowa burial is the better-supported fact.

**Sources:** Lippe emigrant database (Auswanderer Lippe-USA, entry VH2_00A_1156, researcher Simone Quadfasel, source "Verdenhalven /A 1156, Willer"); Find a Grave 232053819 (via Nimble research agent; unverified directly).

## Generation 2: Ernest Frederich Adolphs (1878–1961)

- **Born** October 1878 at Hofdonop/Donop, Lippe.
  - The Lippe database gives **17 Oct**. Find a Grave, the Social Security index and the California Death Index give **27 Oct**. They are the same person (same parents, same emigration); the exact day is unresolved.
- **Parents** confirmed in his Social Security application index: "Friderich H. Adolphs" and "Friderika Stameier." His California death record gives his mother's maiden name as "Stammier."
- **Emigrated 1892**, age about 13, with his father.
- **Married Lillie Ann Ballhagen/Bollhagen**, 28 Apr 1908, Floyd County (Rockford), Iowa.
- **Lived and farmed at University Park, Mahaska County, Iowa.** He worked as a heating-systems engineer at **John Fletcher College** and managed its student work program.
- **Draft registrations:** WWI (1917–18, residence University Park, Mahaska Co.) and WWII "old man's draft" (1942, age 64).
- Around 1952 he was living in **Washougal, Washington** (named in his brother Henry's obituary).
- **Died** 2 May 1961, **Sacramento, California**; **buried University Park Cemetery**, Mahaska Co., Iowa (Find a Grave 69757444).

### Lillie Ann Ballhagen (1883–1932)
- Born 15 Aug 1883, Benezette, Butler County, Iowa. Parents: Theodore Bollhagen and Mary Keager/Krieger.
- Died 10 Jul 1932 in Iowa City; buried University Park Cemetery (Find a Grave 69757561).
- She was about 48 and her youngest child was about 4. Ruby was about 15 when her mother died.

## The adoption: Ruby's son, born in Montezuma, Iowa

Family account (from the researcher): Ruby gave birth to a son, the researcher's father, **believed to have been born in Montezuma, Poweshiek County, Iowa**. He was adopted into the **Stone family**, and the adoption reportedly went **through a church or church agency**.

How this fits the record trail:
- Montezuma is about 25 miles north of University Park, where Ruby grew up. It is the Poweshiek County seat and had the county hospital and courthouse. A birth there fits a young woman from the Oskaloosa area.
- Church-run child-placing agencies were common in Iowa then. In the 1920s the state licensed Lutheran agencies such as the Lutheran Home Finding Society (Fort Dodge), the Iowa Evangelical Lutheran Orphanage (Waverly) and the Iowa Lutheran Children's Home (Stanton), plus Catholic and other agencies. The agency's denomination may echo the Adolphs or Stone family's church.

### Best route: the original birth certificate (Iowa law since 2022)
- Iowa Code § 144.24A (HF 855, effective 1 Jan 2022) lets an **adult adoptee born in Iowa** get a **non-certified copy of their original, pre-adoption birth certificate** from Iowa HHS Vital Records. The fee is $15 and processing takes about 6+ weeks.
- **If the adoptee has died**, his spouse or an adult relative within the second degree (this includes his **children**) may apply, with a certified copy of his death certificate.
- Birthparents could ask for their name to be **redacted**. **Ruby died in 1998, decades before that option existed, so she could not have filed one.** A birth father who was alive in 2021–22 could have.
- The original certificate should name **Ruby as mother**. It may also name the **birth father**, and it gives the hospital or address.

### Other records
- **The agency's case file** (often names both birth parents and describes the circumstances): ask Lutheran Services in Iowa or Catholic Charities (Diocese of Des Moines or Davenport) about post-adoption search. Otherwise, find out which agency handled the placement. The adoptive family's church is a good clue.
- **Court adoption file**, Poweshiek County District Court (or the county where the Stones lived): sealed; opening it requires petitioning the court.
- **Iowa Mutual Consent Voluntary Adoption Registry** (Iowa HHS): matches adoptees and birth relatives who both register.
- **DNA:** a test by the researcher, or ideally the adoptee if living, will match Adolphs/Ballhagen descendants if the link is biological. It could also identify the birth father's family.

## Next steps (most promising first)

1. **Ruby's Social Security application (SS-5).** Request it from the SSA through a FOIA request for a deceased person. It shows her parents, birthplace and the address and employer at the time she applied, often with her married name.
2. **Ruby's Illinois death certificate** (Cook County Clerk, 1998). It names the informant (often a child), the funeral home and the burial place.
3. **Chicago Tribune / Sun-Times death notices, late Nov–early Dec 1998.** Available through ProQuest at most public libraries and through the Chicago Public Library.
4. **1940 and 1950 censuses.** Search for Ruby Smith, born about 1916 in Iowa, with husband Arthur, especially in Chicago or Mahaska/Floyd Co., Iowa. The 1950 census is free on FamilySearch and the National Archives site.
5. **Iowa marriage records.** Serenus's marriage is indexed. Search Ruby Adolphs / Arthur Smith in Mahaska County (Clerk of Court, Oskaloosa) for about 1935–1940.
6. **For the adoption itself:**
   - If your father was born in **Illinois**, the Illinois Adoption Registry and the non-certified original birth certificate process are open to adult adoptees. In some cases surviving descendants of deceased adoptees can apply too; check current rules with the Illinois Department of Public Health.
   - **DNA** (AncestryDNA is best for US matches). Matches to descendants of Ernest's other children (Ehlert, Tisinger, Adolphs of Dudley NC, Petoskey MI) or to Floyd County Adolphs/Daake families would confirm the biological link.
7. **Germany:** church registers of **Ev. Kirche Donop** (Blomberg) and **Talle** are on **Archion.de**. The Landesarchiv NRW, Abteilung Ostwestfalen-Lippe (Detmold) holds the emigration files.
8. ***History of Floyd County, Iowa* (1917), pp. 720–722**: free on Internet Archive / Google Books. Henry E. Adolphs's biography likely describes the family's arrival.
